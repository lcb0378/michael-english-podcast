女生: 从露西的大花园，我们来看看向日葵自己的秘密：它真的会跟着太阳转吗？答案可能会让你大吃一惊，快听！

男生: Now: do sunflowers really follow the sun? The answer is yes — but only when they are young! A young sunflower turns its face from east in the morning to west in the evening, just as you turn your forehead toward the warm morning sun. At night, it quietly turns back to the east, ready for sunrise.

How does it move? It does not spin like a machine. The secret is in the stem: water and liquid food travel up inside the stem, and the tissue on the shaded side grows faster, so the stem bends slowly toward the light. Scientists call this daily dance heliotropism: a slow, steady pulse, like the plant's own inner clock.

But the most surprising aspect: when the flower grows up, it stops dancing! A mature sunflower stays facing east all day. Why? An east-facing flower warms up fast in the morning, and warm flowers attract more bees. Scientists conducted an experiment: they turned some flowers to face west, and the east-facing flowers attracted five times as many pollinators! So the sunflower's trick is not just following the sun — it is knowing when to stop.
