男生: In Beijing, there is a special coffee shop. There is no human barista inside — a robot makes all the coffee, all day and all night. You tap your order on a screen. Let me clarify one thing: it is not a big brewery with huge tanks. It is a small, neat machine that makes one cup at a time. It follows the same standard steps every time: grinding the beans, pouring hot water, adding milk, and handing you the cup.

Cup by cup, the numbers accumulate: on its opening day, the little shop served 202 cups in one hour — more than three cups every minute! Visitors often remark on how fast it is, and a group of morning customers appreciate it most at night, when every other shop is closed. In total, the robot can work all night without feeling tired. 男生: Now let's fly into the quiet night. When the dark curtain of night falls, the owl wakes up. And here is a surprise: an owl can turn its head about 270 degrees — almost all the way around — without moving its body!

Why? First, count the bones: we have seven small bones in our necks, but an owl has fourteen — twice as many! More bones mean more room to turn.

Second, an owl cannot move its eyes: its big eyes are fixed in their sockets, like cameras that cannot look left or right. So the head must do all the turning, and the owl can look behind itself while its body stays still.

But wait — what causes the owl's brain to keep working when its neck twists so far? In our necks, such a twist would squeeze the blood vessels. The owl has a clever design: its neck bones have little air pockets, and its blood vessels have extra room and backup paths, so blood keeps flowing to the brain. It is like a road with side streets: if one is blocked, the blood takes another.

So on a dark night, the owl turns its head, listens, and hears a tiny mouse moving in the grass. Dinner is served — and the mouse never saw the owl move.
