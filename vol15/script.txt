男生: Hello, explorers, welcome back to Michael's Morning Listening! This is our fifteenth show! Today we visit a library whose star worker was a cat. Then we look up at the Moon and learn a secret: our oldest friend in the sky is quietly walking away. A cat, books, and the Moon — let's go!

女生: 先钻进一座小图书馆。在美国爱荷华州的一个小镇上，图书馆的还书口里，曾经被人塞进来一只小猫。它是怎么一步步变成“猫馆长”的呢？听好啦。

男生: On the coldest night of the year, somebody pushed a tiny kitten through the book-return slot of a library in Iowa, America. In the morning, librarian Vicki opened the metal box — and found a baby cat, eight weeks old, shaking like a leaf. She gave him a warm bath and a saucer of milk.

The staff had a decision: could a cat live in a library? They named him Dewey — Dewey Readmore Books! 'Dewey' comes from the Dewey Decimal System, the code that gives every book a place on the shelf. And 'read more books' — what a perfect name for a library cat!

Dewey soon had a job: official greeter. Every morning he waited by the door, walked readers between the shelves, and slept in the sunniest chair. Within a fortnight, the town knew him. Children read to him, and lonely people came to hold him. He always knew who needed a friend.

Soon visitors drove hundreds of miles to meet the famous cat. One day the quiet library erupted with camera flashes — reporters! Dewey's story travelled the world and became a much-loved book. For nineteen years he lived among the books. That tiny kitten was now the world's most famous library cat.

女生: 从安静的图书馆，我们抬头望向夜空。第二位主角大得多，也远得多——它就是月亮。科学家说，它正在以每年几厘米的速度，慢慢地离开地球。这是真的吗？他们是怎么量出来的呢？

男生: Now look up: our second story stars the Moon — and here is its secret: the Moon is leaving us. Do not worry: it leaves slowly — about three point eight centimetres a year, as fast as your fingernails grow.

How can scientists be sure? Astronauts left mirrors behind on the Moon. Scientists fire laser beams at the mirrors and time the light's return trip. Light's speed never changes, so the time tells the distance — about three hundred and eighty-four thousand kilometres. That is how they observe and determine the Moon's escape, millimetre by millimetre.

Why does the Moon drift away? Blame the tides. The Moon pulls Earth's oceans into two bulges. Our planet spins fast and drags those bulges ahead of the Moon, and the bulges pull the Moon forward, like a hand pushing a swing. So the Moon climbs into a wider orbit, Earth spins a little slower, and our days grow longer.

Long ago, the Moon hung closer. It looked enormous, the tides were giant, and a day was shorter than twenty-four hours. The Moon will drift for millions of years — but tonight it is still our big, bright neighbour. Wave when you see it!

女生: 学知识时间到！今天的俚语，和第二位主角严丝合缝：over the moon。字面意思是“飞到月亮上头去了”，说的是开心到飞起。听听男主播的讲解和例句。

男生: Here's today's idiom: 'over the moon.' If you are over the moon, you are extremely happy — so happy you could jump over the Moon! When Dewey's librarians heard that people drove for hours to meet their cat, they were over the moon. Here's an example: 'I won the reading contest, and I was over the moon!' What makes you feel over the moon?
