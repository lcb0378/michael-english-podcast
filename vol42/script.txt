男生: First, let's look up at the sky. At a giant kite festival, thousands of kites fill the air. There are dragons and whales, sea turtles and astronauts, all floating high above the ground. But the biggest star of the show is an octopus — a giant octopus kite!

One famous octopus kite is eighty meters long. That is longer than two blue whales, nose to tail! It took its maker six months to finish. It is so big that a team of at least six people must work together to fly it. At minimum, six people hold the lines, and sometimes they even need a car to pull it into the air!

When the wind catches it, the long arms wave and dance, and the whole sky turns into a colorful curtain. Visitors look up and yell with joy. 女生: 从热闹的天空，我们潜入凉爽的河里。河马晒着太阳，皮肤会慢慢变粉，这是怎么回事？难道河马真的会流“血汗”吗？快听！

男生: Now let's dive into the river and meet a very big animal with a very strange secret: the hippopotamus. When a hippo comes out of the water on a sunny day, its skin slowly turns pink. For a long time, people thought hippos sweated blood! Let me clarify: it is not blood, and it is not really sweat, either.

Hippos do not have sweat glands like we do. Instead, tiny glands under their skin make a clear, oily fluid. In just a few minutes in the air and sun, the fluid turns pink and then red. Scientists found two special pigments inside it: a red one called hipposudoric acid, and an orange one called norhipposudoric acid.

Together, they work like a natural sunscreen. They soak up the sun's ultraviolet rays, so the hippo's sensitive skin does not burn. The same fluid also fights bacteria, so small cuts from life in a muddy river do not easily get infected. And because it is oily, it keeps the skin soft and wet, like a built-in moisturizer.

Hippos spend most of the day in the water to stay cool. But when they come out onto the bank in the sun, their own pink sunscreen is already on the job. You have to appreciate how clever nature is!
