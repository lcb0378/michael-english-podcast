男生: Welcome back to Michael's Morning Listening! Today we meet a real super-sniffer with the best nose in the business. Then we blast off to Mars, where a brave spacecraft will grab a piece of the moon Phobos. Let's jump in!

女生: 先说说这位"超级嗅探员"。它曾经是山地搜救犬，如今换了一份更特别的工作——连实验室都要花大力气才能找到的东西，它用鼻子就能闻出来！

男生: Meet Sgiath, a six-year-old black Labrador from the Scottish Highlands. She began as a mountain rescue dog — but today she has a different job.

Sgiath is now believed to be the first dog in the world trained to smell a dangerous sea creature — the carpet sea squirt, also known as "sea vomit." This creature came from abroad, from waters near Japan, and has spread across harbours in Britain. It grows like a dark cloud over oyster farms and smothers the native sea life underneath.

Finding it early is important, but it is not easy. Scientists must wear thick gloves and use DNA tests to spot it. Sgiath needs only her nose. Her handler, Dr. Texa Sim, says the first time Sgiath tried, she went straight to it — all by herself!

At low tide, she works with Dr. Sim along the shore. When she finds something, she never barks — she just goes still and stares. Her reward is food — she is a greedy girl!

All this clever nose work won Sgiath the Animal of the Year award from IFAW, the International Fund for Animal Welfare. She shows us the true value of a great nose — and a great dog!

女生: 从苏格兰的高地到海边，Sgiath 用鼻子守护着海洋。接下来我们要去的地方更远——火星！日本派出的飞船 MMX，要去火星的月亮上"取土"带回地球。

男生: Now let's fly to Mars — not the planet, but one of its moons!

Japan has launched a big space mission called MMX, which stands for Martian Moons eXploration. Its goal: bring home a piece of Phobos, the larger of Mars' two moons. Phobos is a lumpy, potato-shaped rock about twenty-two kilometers wide — not a perfect ball at all!

Scientists still do not know where Phobos came from. Was it a passing asteroid caught by Mars long ago? Or was it born from the dust of a giant crash on young Mars? The answer has been hiding behind millions of miles — until now.

MMX will spend about three years flying around Phobos. A small robot arm will dig into the surface twice, collecting at least ten grams of dust and rocks — about the weight of a rabbit! The landing must be as gentle as silk, because gravity on Phobos is very weak. One wrong move, and the spacecraft could bounce right off!

Then a small capsule will house the precious samples for the long trip home. Count the years: it is a five-year journey! Back on Earth, the whole team will be wide awake for an exciting activity — opening the capsule and reading the story in the dust. Those tiny grains may tell us how Mars' moons were born, and how water and life's building blocks traveled around the young solar system.

女生: 学知识时间到！今天的俚语和 Sgiath 的神鼻子有关：have a nose for something。字面意思是"对某事有鼻子"，实际意思是"对某事特别有感觉、有天赋"。听听男主播的讲解和例句。

男生: Here's today's idiom: "have a nose for something." It means you are good at finding or understanding something — like a dog with a great nose!

For example: "My friend Jack has a nose for good stories — he taught himself to write news at twelve." Jack finds stories the way Sgiath finds sea creatures!

So — do you have a nose for something? Maybe you have a nose for music, or a nose for finding lost socks at home!

女生: "have a nose for something" 就是"对某事特别有感觉"。比如：我朋友 Jack 对好故事特别敏感，十二岁就自学写新闻——就像 Sgiath 对海洋生物的鼻子一样灵！你也可以想想：你 have a nose for 什么？是音乐，还是找东西？
