男生: Our first hero stands in London: the famous Tower Bridge, copied in LEGO bricks! They used five million, eight hundred and five thousand, eight hundred and forty-six bricks. It is the largest LEGO sculpture ever built: forty-four metres long and thirteen metres tall! Media cameras came from around the world, and boats sailed past below. Here is its silliest appeal: lay the bricks in one line, and they stretch from London to Paris!
LEGO builders hold another record: the world's longest LEGO bridge. Engineers built one thirty-four metres long, from more than two hundred and five thousand bricks — with no glue at all! Its middle span hung almost seventeen metres across the air. Every brick had to click perfectly: one loose brick could damage the whole bridge.
Then came an awkward moment. As the deck grew heavier, one anchor that held the cables began to tilt! The builders held their breath. Quickly, they piled heavy weights onto the anchor and pressed it firm. When the last plate clicked into place, a huge cheer erupted. A bridge is a quiet service to a city: it carries everyone across, asking nothing back.

男生: Now dive with me into the deep past. Here is a fact that astonishes even scientists: sharks are older than trees! The oldest shark fossils are not bones or teeth, but tiny scales in rocks four hundred and fifty million years old. The significance of those scales is enormous: sharks already swam when the land had no forests — only mosses a few centimetres tall, and no shady tree on any beach.
The first true trees grew about sixty million years later. One, called Wattieza, looked like a giant fern on a stick. So for sixty million years, sharks swam almost alone as kings of the sea, while the world above the waves lay quiet and bare, as if fast asleep.
Since then, sharks have survived five great extinctions. Sea monsters rose and fell, dynasties of dinosaurs came and went — and the sharks just kept swimming. Even their skin is a tool: tiny tooth-like scales cover it, so rough that sailors once stretched it over a board to shave wood smooth, like sandpaper. So remember: a shark is a design older than every forest on Earth!
