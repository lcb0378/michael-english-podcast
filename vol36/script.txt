男生: In Argentina, there is a boy named Faustino Oro, called the "Messi of chess." He did not learn it at school. He started playing online games at home at seven, while everyone stayed home. His father made an account for him, and soon he played everywhere — after breakfast and before bed. The bond between father and son grew with every game they watched together.

Every weekday, homework first, then chess. He learned fast — faster than a balloon rising. He studies the board like an accountant: every piece counts. In the course of a year, he played hundreds of games online. Win by win, he accumulated points and climbed higher. Meanwhile, older players noticed him. The statistics tell the story: he became the youngest player ever to pass 2300 points, and at only ten, he became the youngest International Master ever. The chess federation approved his title, and fans everywhere knew his name.

But his most famous game was on a computer screen. In a fast online game, ten-year-old Faustino beat Magnus Carlsen, a former world champion! His facial expression stayed calm. His coach says: a calm mind wins more games than fast hands. When he lost, he did not mourn for long — he looked at the board and asked, "What can I learn?" The road was not always easy, but game after game, he kept going. And now, at twelve, he is a Grandmaster. What a star!

女生: 从棋盘跳到树枝上！第二位主角是变色龙，它的舌头比身体还长，出手快如闪电。它是怎么做到的？快听！

男生: Now climb into the trees and meet the chameleon, the quiet king of hide-and-seek. A chameleon changes color and moves its eyes in two directions at once. But its greatest trick is its tongue. A big chameleon's tongue can be twice its body length, and a tiny one's two and a half times! Imagine your tongue reaching across the whole classroom!

How does it work? The tongue sits folded like an accordion over a small bone. When it sees a cricket, muscles pull back slowly, like drawing a bow. Then — snap! The tongue shoots out faster than lightning, in less than a tenth of a second. Stretchy tissues work like a spring, storing power and letting go all at once. The tip is covered with sticky mucus, thicker than honey, so the cricket sticks fast. It can even catch a bug as heavy as one-third of its own weight!

Then the tongue pulls the meal back — dinner is served, without moving a step. Scientists love this trick: it works in hot deserts and cold mountains alike. So remember: slow feet, quick tongue — the chameleon's secret!
