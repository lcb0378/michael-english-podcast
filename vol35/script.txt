女生: 先说海龟。在澳大利亚的一片海滩上，有人发现了一只奄奄一息的绿海龟，身上爬满了藤壶和水蛭。它后来怎么样了？竖起耳朵听好啦。

男生: On a beach in Australia, a walker found a green sea turtle in big trouble. She was tired and thin, and her shell was covered with barnacles, algae, and even leeches. Rescuers from a sea animal hospital took her in and gave her a name: Kalani.

The doctors found a cut on her back flipper, and her body was very weak. For six months, the team cleaned her shell, gave her medicine, and watched her day and night. Slowly, Kalani began to recover. She swam faster, ate more, and soon she was the boss of her tank! The doctors were delighted: after a final, valid health check, this turtle was ready to go home.

On release day, Kalani swam into the waves with four other rescued turtles. But her story also carries a warning. Plastic bags in the sea can choke a turtle that mistakes one for food, and old fishing nets can trap flippers. In some countries, federal laws now protect sea turtles, but the best protection is simple: keep plastic out of the ocean.

女生: 从海龟的故事，我们潜到潮水退去的礁石上。那里趴着一种看起来像星星的动物——海星。它没有大脑，也没有血，那它是怎么活下来的？快听！

男生: Now meet the starfish — although scientists prefer to call it a sea star, because it is not a fish at all! It is an echinoderm, a cousin of sea urchins and sand dollars. And here is the strangest part: a starfish has no brain. The brain is absent. The blood is absent too. So how does it live, move, and eat?

Instead of a brain, it has a nerve ring around its mouth, with nerves running down each arm. The arms are in charge! And instead of blood, it uses seawater. Water comes in through a tiny sieve plate on its back, called the madreporite, and flows through canals into hundreds of delicate little tube feet. The water pressure fills the feet and stretches them out, and muscles pull them back in. That is how a starfish walks, grips rocks, and even pries open a clam.

And when the tide goes out, a starfish on the rocks is exposed to the air and sun — but its tough skin and tube feet help it hold on until the water returns. Even more amazing, if it loses an arm, it can grow a new one. No brain, no blood — and yet it has lived in the oceans for hundreds of millions of years. Isn't that awesome?
