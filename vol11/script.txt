Today we meet a runner with legs of metal in Beijing. Then we dive under the sea, to watch an octopus paint dreams on its skin. No flying saucers — but there will be running and dreams. Ready? Let's go!

女生: 先来到北京的马路上。几千名人类跑者旁边，站着二十一个机器人——这是世界上第一场专门给机器人办的半程马拉松。它们跑得怎么样？中间有没有摔跤？听好啦。

男生: On your marks, get set... go! In Beijing, twenty-one humanoid robots lined up for a race: the world's first half-marathon for robots — twenty-one kilometres beside thousands of human runners. The robots came in all sizes: some no taller than a small child, others as tall as a basketball player. Thousands of fans came to observe. One robot wore running shoes. One wore boxing gloves! One robot fell on its face, waved its arms, and got up again. The winner was a tall robot called Tiangong Ultra: one point eight metres of metal and wires, fifty-five kilograms. It crossed the finish line in two hours, forty minutes and forty-two seconds, stopping only three times for a new battery. The fastest human needed one hour and two minutes — so the robots have homework to do! Building a running robot means compromise: long legs run fast, but they drink power. The engineers were determined anyway. Why race? Not for medals, and not for justice — every robot that finished was a hero. When Tiangong Ultra crossed the line, the crowd erupted with cheers.

女生: 从热闹的马路，我们潜进安静的大海。第二位主角是一只小章鱼。它睡着以后，身上会发生一件神奇的事，科学家看了都觉得不可思议。

男生: Now, from the noisy road to a quiet sea. In Japan, scientists watched twenty-nine sleeping octopuses — and found a surprise. An octopus sleeps in two ways. In quiet sleep, it lies pale and still, like a stone. But about once an hour, it slips into active sleep for one minute — and the show begins! Its skin flashes red, brown and gold. Its eyes roll. Its eight arms, like the branches of a tree, twitch and wave as it breathes faster. Under the skin sit tiny colour cells called chromatophores, turning the colours up and down like a dial. Stranger still, its brain buzzes with the patterns it uses when awake and hiding — or hunting. So scientists think the octopus may be replaying its day: it may be dreaming! When the team kept the octopuses awake too long, they fell into active sleep sooner, and more often — like you after a busy day. And after a meal, an octopus rests to digest its food... and perhaps to dream about it. The team hopes this helps conservation: the better we understand sea animals, the better we can protect their homes.

男生: Here's today's idiom: 'break the ice.' Long ago, ships broke the ice on frozen rivers so other boats could pass. Today, when strangers meet, things can feel cold and quiet — until someone tells a joke or asks a question. That breaks the ice! Example: 'At my new school, I broke the ice by sharing my sandwiches.' Who will you break the ice with this week?
