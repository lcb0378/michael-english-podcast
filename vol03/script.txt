男生: Now let's meet an animal famous for its long neck — but today we are going to talk about its brain instead.

At a zoo in Barcelona, Spain, scientists wanted to test whether giraffes can do math. They showed four giraffes some boxes with carrots inside — a giraffe's favorite snack. Here is how the game worked. The scientist put carrots into two boxes where the giraffes could see them, then closed the boxes. Next, he secretly moved more carrots into one of the closed boxes. The giraffes could not see inside anymore. Then he asked them to choose: which box has more carrots now?

To get it right, the giraffes had to remember the first amounts, then add the new carrots in their heads — without ever seeing the final pile. And guess what? They picked the right box about sixty-eight times out of a hundred — much better than random guessing! Two giraffes, Nuru and Njano, were the star students.

But here is the twist: the giraffes were good at addition, but terrible at subtraction — exactly like most human kids! So the next time math homework feels hard, remember: even a giraffe finds subtraction tricky.

This discovery is important because it shows that number skills are not just for humans, monkeys, and parrots. The animal kingdom is full of secret mathematicians!
