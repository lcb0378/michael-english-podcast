女生: 欢迎来到 Michael 的早听晚读！我是 Cherry。今天我们要认识两位“慢冠军”：一条活了四十多年的小金鱼，和一只一睡就能睡好几年的蜗牛。一个教我们耐心，一个教我们等待的艺术。准备好了吗？出发！

男生: Hello, young explorers, and welcome back to Michael's Morning Listening! Today we meet two slow champions: a little goldfish that outlived most dogs, and a snail that can sleep for years. One teaches us patience; the other, the art of waiting. Let's go!

女生: 先来讲金鱼的故事。一般金鱼能活十到十五年，可有一条小金鱼，竟然活了四十三年，还创了世界纪录！它是怎么被赢回家的，又是怎么活这么久的？竖起耳朵听好啦。

男生: Most goldfish live ten to fifteen years. But one little goldfish named Tish lived forty-three years — a world record! A seven-year-old boy named Peter won him at a fair, in a roll-a-coin game. Peter's mother Hilda then looked after Tish for most of his long life. His vivid orange scales slowly turned silver, like an old man's hair.

The caption under his photo, on a page of the record book, says he lived in a simple bowl, with fresh water and quiet days. He even survived a great escape: one day he jumped out of his bowl, but Hilda found him in time, and he swam away as if nothing had happened. The record book makes no discrimination between big animals and small ones — a tiny fish can be a champion too.

Tish was not alone. A cat named Creme Puff lived thirty-eight years — longer than most dogs! Tish's story appeared in every format: in books, in newspapers, and on television. Tish became an ambassador for all small pets: with patience and care, even the smallest friend can stay a very long time.

女生: 从鱼缸边，我们走到花园里。有一只小蜗牛，天气一干，它就把自己关进壳里，一睡就是好几个月，甚至好几年！它到底是怎么做到的？科学家给这种本事起了一个专门的名字。

男生: Now meet a slower champion: the garden snail. On a warm day, a snail slides along a leaf, leaving a silver trail. But when hot, dry days come and the garden dries up, the snail does something amazing. It climbs into its shell and seals the door with a layer of dried slime, like closing a little gate. Inside, its body slows almost to a stop. It does not eat. It does not move. It waits.

This deep sleep is not really sleep. Scientists call it estivation — a summer sleep for dry times. In this state, a snail can wait for months. In the driest places, some snails have waited for years — up to three years! A pedestrian may walk right past the quiet shell at the bottom of the garden wall. The snail is deaf to the footsteps and the wind, but it feels the change in the air.

Then, at last, the rain comes. A fine spray falls on the garden. The snail wakes, opens its little gate, and slides out into the morning light. The sun rises in the east, and the garden comes alive again.

女生: 学知识时间到！今天的俚语，和拳击比赛有关：saved by the bell。字面意思是“被铃声救了”，说的是在最后一刻被人或事救了一把。听听男主播的讲解和例句。

男生: Here's today's idiom: 'saved by the bell.' In a boxing match, when a tired boxer is almost beaten, the bell rings to end the round — and he is saved by the bell! We use it when something saves us at the very last moment. Here's an example: 'Leo forgot his lines in the school play, but he was saved by the bell when the lunch bell rang and everyone erupted in cheers.' When were you saved by the bell?

女生: 今天的故事都听完啦？我们认识了创纪录的金鱼蒂什，又看蜗牛用一扇小小的黏液门熬过干旱。现在轮到你们动脑筋啦，听听男主播的问题，想好了可以说给爸爸妈妈听。

男生: Let's think about our stories. Tish lived forty-three years, and a snail can wait three years for rain. Now, two questions for you. First: if you had a pet, what would you do to help it live a long, happy life? Second: if you could sleep for three years, what would you most want to see when you wake up? Talk about it with your family!

女生: 好啦，今天的早听晚读就到这里。愿你像蒂什一样有耐心，像蜗牛一样懂得等待。我们明天早上见！

男生: That's all for today, young explorers. Be patient like Tish, and brave like a snail. And remember: sometimes help comes just in time — saved by the bell! See you tomorrow morning!
