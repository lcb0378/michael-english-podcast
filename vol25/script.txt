男生: Now meet a frog that can almost disappear. The glassfrog lives in the rainforests of Central and South America. It is small and green, and its flesh is so clear you can see its heart and stomach right through it — like looking through glass. By day, it sleeps on the underside of a leaf, where hungry birds could easily spot it. So how does it stay safe? Scientists found the answer with a special camera that can see blood moving inside the body. When the glassfrog sleeps, it packs almost ninety percent of its red blood cells into its liver, hidden behind tiny mirror-like crystals. With the red cells out of the way, light passes through the frog's body, and it becomes two or three times more see-through — it simply fades into the leaf. Their primary job, carrying oxygen, can wait until the frog wakes up. When it wakes, the cells rush back into the blood in seconds, and it can jump away. Most animals could never do this safely — but the glassfrog does it every day, and its trick may teach doctors new ideas about blood.
