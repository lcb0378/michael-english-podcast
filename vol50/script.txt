女生: 从十个老朋友的故事里回来，我们追踪今天的主角：声音。它从哪里来，又是怎么钻进你的耳朵、变成你听到的世界的？快听！

男生: Every sound begins with a shake. Hit a drum, and its skin shakes back and forth. That shake is called a vibration, and it pushes the air into a wave. Our approach is simple: follow that wave, step by step, all the way into your ear. First, your outer ear catches the wave and funnels it down the ear canal to a thin piece of tissue called the eardrum. The wave makes the eardrum shake!

Next, the shake reaches three tiny bones — the hammer, the anvil and the stirrup. They are the smallest bones in your body, and they conduct the shake onward, making it stronger. Then comes the most amazing aspect: a snail-shaped room full of fluid, called the cochlea. Inside, thousands of tiny hair cells bend like grass in the wind, and their bending turns the shake into electrical signals. Those signals race along the auditory nerve to your brain, and your brain reads them as a sparrow's song, a friend's laugh, or music. Sound moves as a pulse through the air at about 343 metres every second.

And here is a fun fact: sound needs something to travel through. In empty space, with no air at all, there is no sound — that is why space is silent! So remember the journey: shake, wave, eardrum, bones, cochlea, brain — all in less than a blink!

男生: Today's idiom is "your guess is as good as mine." It means "I don't know either!" For example: if you ask me how many songs a sparrow can sing, your guess is as good as mine! Try it: when a friend asks you something nobody knows, just smile and say: your guess is as good as mine!
