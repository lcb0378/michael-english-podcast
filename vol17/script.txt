男生: Lunch is served — by drone! On the Badaling Great Wall near Beijing, hungry climbers order on their phones, and a drone brings lunch through the sky. This part of the wall has no shops at all. Walking down for a drink takes about fifty minutes. The drone shaves that long walk down to five. Here is how it works. At the South Ninth Tower, visitors scan a code and order drinks, snacks, or a sandwich on the Meituan app. Workers pack the food, the drone lifts off, and minutes later it lands softly by the tower. It can carry about two kilograms. The wall is truly ancient: dynasty after dynasty of builders worked on it for two thousand years, and nobody wants the crowds to damage it. On the way back, the drones carry the visitors' rubbish down. No new shops, no mess left behind. Drones can rush medicine to a sick climber, faster than legs on those steep, awkward steps. The idea has a simple appeal: lunch without the long climb. When the first lunches flew in, climbers were astonished, and the crowd erupted in cheers. Videos spread quickly on social media. A small service, with real significance.

男生: Now meet a dancer: the honeybee. When a bee finds flowers full of nectar among the leaves, she returns home and tells her sisters where the food is — not with words, but with a dance. Karl von Frisch, a scientist from Austria, watched bees for years and cracked the code. It later won a Nobel Prize. Inside the dark hive, she dances on the honeycomb in a figure of eight. The middle of the dance is a straight run, and while she runs, she waggles her body from side to side. This waggle run carries the message. Her sisters feel it with their feelers, reading the dance like a map. The angle of the run gives the direction. Straight up means: fly towards the sun. Turn left or right, and the flowers sit at that same angle from the sun. The waggle's length gives the distance: short means near, long means far — about one second of waggling for every kilometre. Von Frisch tested his idea with a saucer of sugar water. He moved it step by step; dancers changed their angles, and their sisters flew straight to the new place. Scientists from Austria to America still study the dance. Don't worry when a bee hurries past — she is rushing home to dance the news.
