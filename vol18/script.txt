女生: 欢迎来到 Michael 的早听晚读！我是 Cherry。今天是我们的第十八期！第一位主角，是一颗小小的辣椒，辣得全世界只有五个人敢整颗吃下去；第二位主角，是一杯热水——它居然能比冷水更快结冰。一火一冰，都是谜。准备好了吗？出发！

男生: Hello, young explorers, and welcome back to Michael's Morning Listening! This is our eighteenth show together! Today, two hot mysteries. First, the hottest pepper ever — so hot that only five people have ever eaten a whole one. Then, a kitchen puzzle: can hot water turn into ice faster than cold water? Let's go!

女生: 先来说这颗辣椒。它个头不大，脾气却大得惊人，科学家给它量出的辣度创下了世界纪录。它到底有多辣？又是谁种出来的呢？听好啦。

男生: Meet Pepper X, the hottest chilli pepper of all. It grows in South Carolina, in America, under green leaves. Its grower is Ed Currie, a pepper scientist with a dream. He mixed pepper plants again and again — and after ten years, Pepper X was born.

How hot is it? Scientists measure pepper heat with the Scoville scale. A jalapeño pepper reads about five thousand. Pepper X? The lab reading astonished everyone: nearly two million, seven hundred thousand! That is more than five hundred times hotter than a jalapeño. Guinness gave Pepper X the crown: hottest pepper ever measured.

Why is Pepper X so hot? The fire lives in capsaicin, a chemical in the white skin that holds the seeds. Pepper X is small and wrinkly, and the wrinkles give that skin more room to grow, so it makes much more fire.

Only five people have eaten a whole one. They sweat, cry, and the media film it. Do not worry — the fire is a trick. Capsaicin shouts “Fire!” to your nerves, but it cannot really burn or damage you. Water cannot wash it away. Milk can — milk carries fat.

女生: 从火热的辣椒，我们跳进冰凉的冰箱。第二位主角是坦桑尼亚的一位中学生，他在做冰淇淋的时候发现了一件怪事：还冒着热气的冰淇淋糊，居然比凉的先冻好。

男生: Now fly to Tanzania, in Africa, to meet a schoolboy, Erasto Mpemba. His class made ice cream from boiled milk and sugar. One day, with the freezer nearly full, Mpemba pushed his hot mix straight in — no waiting for it to cool.

Later, Mpemba checked the freezer — and stared. His hot mix was ice cream, but his classmate's cold mix was still soft! He asked his teacher why, but the teacher just laughed: “Mpemba's physics!” The whole class erupted in laughter. The question stayed in his head.

Years later, a scientist, Dr Denis Osborne, visited Mpemba's new school. He asked again: “Two cups of water, one at thirty-five degrees and one at one hundred — why does the hotter one freeze first?” Osborne returned to his laboratory, tested the idea again, and was astonished: the student was right. They published the answer together, and the effect now carries Mpemba's name.

So why can hot water win the race to ice? Scientists still argue about the answer. Hot water steams, so some of it flies away — less water is left to freeze. It also swims in slow circles, carrying heat out faster. But it does not always work. It needs the right cups, the right temperatures — the right conditions. Then hot really can beat cold!

女生: 学知识时间到！今天的俚语，和两个故事都有关，叫 in hot water。字面意思是“在热水里”，可不是让你去泡澡哦。听听男主播的讲解和例句。

男生: Here's today's idiom: “in hot water.” But nobody is cooking, and nobody is taking a bath! If you are in hot water, you are in trouble. Here's an example: “Tom forgot his homework again, so now he is in hot water with his teacher.” Poor Mpemba asked about hot water — he was never in it!
