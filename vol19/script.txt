女生: 欢迎来到 Michael 的早听晚读！我是 Cherry。这是我们的第十九期！今天的第一位主角，是一位一百零六岁的老爷爷，他从三千米的高空一跃而下，要把失去的世界纪录赢回来！第二位主角住在澳大利亚，个头不大，却有一项全世界独一无二的本领：它拉的便便，是方块形的！准备好了吗？出发！

男生: Hello, young explorers, and welcome back to Michael's Morning Listening! This is our nineteenth show together! Today we meet two record-breakers. One will astonish you: he is one hundred and six years old, and he jumps out of planes. The other is small, round, and furry — and it makes the world's strangest poop. Ready? Let's go!

女生: 先说老爷爷。他叫阿尔·布拉什克，住在美国的得克萨斯州。一百岁生日那天，家人送了他一份大礼——人生第一次跳伞！从那以后，他就爱上了天空。这一次，一位瑞典奶奶把他的纪录抢走了，他微微一笑：我要把它赢回来。他能成功吗？听好啦。

男生: Meet Al Blaschke from Texas — the world's bravest great-grandpa. At one hundred and six, Al climbed into a plane, rose nine thousand feet, and jumped out, strapped to an instructor, to win back the record for the oldest tandem skydiver. Below, his family held their breath. Then his parachute opened, and the field erupted in cheers. The media filmed him floating down. At one hundred and three, he had jumped from fourteen thousand feet for his twin grandsons' graduation — and set the record. "That was my dream," he said. But a Swedish grandmother named Rut took the record away. Al smiled and promised to win it back; the governor of Texas, Greg Abbott, promised to jump beside him. It was a close shave: bad weather cancelled the jump four times. Climbing out is awkward work at any age — the service crew checked his straps a dozen times! At last, a clear morning came. Al and the governor jumped together, and the record belonged to Al again. Reporters asked his secret. "If you think you can't, you are underestimating yourself," he said. "Everyone is more capable than they think. They just need to decide to try."

女生: 从几千米的高空，我们落回地面，钻进澳大利亚的灌木丛。第二位主角圆滚滚、毛茸茸，腿短短的，叫袋熊。它有一个谁也学不会的本事——它的便便不是圆的，是方块的，像骰子一样！科学家们琢磨了好久，才解开这个谜。

男生: Now shrink down to Australia, to meet the wombat: a round, furry marsupial with short legs. The wombat's dynasty is ancient — its cousins once included giants as big as a rhinoceros. Today's wombats are smaller, but they hold a record no other animal can claim: they make cube-shaped poop. A wombat's intestine is about ten metres long, and a meal may take fourteen days to cross it, growing drier and drier. Near the end of the tube, the wall is stretchy in some places and stiff in others, so the dry waste is slowly pressed into flat sides and square corners. Scientists Patricia Yang and David Hu solved the mystery: nobody cuts or moulds the cubes — the intestine squeezes them into shape with tens of thousands of tiny squeezes. The significance reached far beyond poop: the discovery won a funny science prize, the Ig Nobel Prize. Why cubes? Wombats talk by smell. They stack the cubes on rocks and logs by their burrows, like little flags: "This home is taken!" A cube cannot roll away, and rain cannot easily damage the message.

女生: 学知识时间到！今天的俚语是 down to earth，字面意思是"回到地面上"。阿尔爷爷敢从那么高的地方跳下来，可他这个人特别脚踏实地、从不吹牛；袋熊呢，天天贴着地面过日子。这个俚语到底夸的是什么样的人？听听男主播的讲解和例句。

男生: Here's today's idiom: "down to earth." It describes someone practical, honest, and friendly, who never shows off. Think of Al: he flies nine thousand feet up, yet stays down to earth, telling everyone, "You can do it too." Here's an example: "Our teacher is really down to earth — she jokes with us and never acts like a star." That's the appeal: simple words for real, honest people.

女生: 今天的故事都听完啦？我们先是陪一百零六岁的阿尔爷爷从飞机上一跃而下，又去澳大利亚看了袋熊的方块便便。现在轮到你们动脑筋啦，听听男主播的问题，想好了可以说给爸爸妈妈听。

男生: Let's think about our stories. A great-grandpa of one hundred and six jumped out of a plane, and a wombat makes perfect cubes. Now, two questions for you. First: what brave thing would you like to try when you are very, very old? Second: if you could leave one special mark on the world, what would it be? Talk about it with your family!

女生: 好啦，今天的早听晚读就到这里。愿你像阿尔爷爷一样勇敢，又像他一样脚踏实地——敢往下跳，也敢慢慢来。我们明天早上见！

男生: That's all for today, young explorers. Be brave like Al, stay down to earth like the wombat — and never say "I can't." See you tomorrow morning!
