女生: 先说长寿猫奶奶。在英国，有一只叫 Flossie 的玳瑁猫，刚刚过了三十岁生日，成了全世界现存最长寿的猫。它的一生可不平凡，竖起耳朵听好啦。

男生: In a quiet house in England lives a very special cat named Flossie. She is a tortoiseshell cat, black and brown, and she has just celebrated her 30th birthday. That makes her the oldest living cat in the world, confirmed by Guinness World Records. The standard lifespan of a pet cat is about 14 years, so Flossie has lived more than twice as long as most cats!

Flossie did not have an easy start: found as a kitten near a hospital, she lived with several different owners. Some cats live in shops, some even in breweries, but Flossie finally found a quiet home with her owner Vicki, who calls it Flossie's retirement home, where she gets whatever she wants.

Flossie cannot hear well now, and her eyesight is weak, but she still enjoys her days: meals, long naps in sunny spots, sometimes peeking out from behind the curtain, and gentle play. Her owner remarked that Flossie is not treated like a record holder at all. She is simply loved, and her family appreciate every day with her. Imagine everyone yelling, "Happy birthday, Flossie!"

女生: 从猫奶奶的生日派对，我们钻进蚕宝宝的小房间，看看它吐丝的本事。一只小小的蚕宝宝，到底能织出多长的丝线？快听！

男生: Now, from a birthday cat, let's meet a tiny worker with a giant talent: the silkworm, a moth's caterpillar, eating only mulberry leaves. When fully grown, it stops eating and spins its cocoon, a soft case where it will change into a moth.

Here is the amazing part: the cocoon is spun from a single, unbroken thread of silk. Let me clarify: it is not many short pieces joined together. It is one continuous thread, at a minimum 300 metres long; some cocoons hold up to about 900 metres!

How? Inside its body, the silkworm makes a liquid protein. It comes out through a tiny tube near its mouth and hardens in the air into a fine thread, about 10 micrometres wide, far thinner than a human hair. The worm moves its head in a figure-eight dance, laying down layer after layer for two or three days until the cocoon is finished.

In total, thousands of cocoons make just one kilogram of silk. In ancient China, silk was so precious that only emperors, ministers, and rich families could wear it.

男生: Today's idiom is "costs an arm and a leg." It means something is very expensive. For example: That silk dress costs an arm and a leg, because it took thousands of silkworms to make it! Flossie's birthday cake, though, did not cost an arm and a leg: she is happiest with simple food and a sunny nap. So it means a lot of money, but not really your arm or your leg, of course! Try it this week: tell your family about something that costs an arm and a leg.
