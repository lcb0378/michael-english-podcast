女生: 欢迎来到 Michael 的早听晚读！我是 Cherry。今天的第一位主角，是一串等了整整一亿两千万年才被人发现的恐龙脚印——发现它的人，只是位在溪边散步的游客！第二位主角，是一只侏罗纪的小胖子鱿鱼，胖得连科学家都忍不住笑。竖起耳朵，我们出发啦！

男生: Hello, young explorers, and welcome back to Michael's Morning Listening! Today our first story begins with one photo on a phone, and with footprints that waited one hundred and twenty million years to be found. Then we dive into an ancient sea to meet the fattest little squid of Jurassic times. Ready? Let's go!

女生: 先说说这串神奇的脚印。在泰国一座国家公园里，一位名叫 Pichai 的游客沿着小溪散步，在小路边的砂岩上看见了几个奇怪的大印子。他掏出手机拍下照片，发到网上问大家：这像不像恐龙脚印？没想到，这一问，问出了一个大发现。

男生: Our story starts in Phu Phrabat National Park in Thailand. A visitor named Pichai was walking along a small stream when he saw strange, deep marks pressed into the sandstone beside the path. The marks looked like giant footprints. He took a photo with his phone and posted it online with a question: "Do these look like dinosaur footprints to you?"

Park rangers came the very next day to measure the marks. Then dinosaur experts studied them carefully — and confirmed the exciting news. The marks are real! They are six footprints of a meat-eating dinosaur, made about one hundred and twenty million years ago.

There is no skeleton and no bones in the rock — only footprints, like a short message the dinosaur left behind. The soft ground kept the prints, the rock preserved them, and the stream hid them for ages. Only when the water went down did the footprints appear again. Scientists believe the dinosaur was walking here long ago, perhaps looking for food or water. One walk, one photo — and a dinosaur stepped back into the world!

女生: 从恐龙的脚印边，我们一头扎进侏罗纪的大海！第二位主角生活在一亿六千万年前，是一只鱿鱼的远房亲戚。它的同类大多苗条得像子弹，它却胖得像个小木桶，科学家还给它起了个搞笑的外号。

男生: Now let's meet the belemnites — squid cousins from the time of the dinosaurs. They are extinct today, but their hard inner shells turned into fossils, and scientists find them all over the world. Most belemnite fossils are thin and pointed, like little bullets, only one or two centimetres wide.

But in Wyoming, in the United States, scientists found a very different one. Its name is Wyoteuthis, and it lived about one hundred and sixty million years ago, in Jurassic seas. Its shell was shaped like a little barrel — and six centimetres wide! That makes it the fattest, chunkiest belemnite ever found. In real life, the animal was about sixty centimetres long.

A team from the University of Manchester studied the funny fossil. Some scientists even joke that fossils like this look like "squid butts". A fat little squid, a silly nickname — and a real puzzle for science: why did this one grow so wide, when all its cousins stayed slim?

女生: 学知识时间到！今天的俚语，和第一篇故事严丝合缝：follow in someone's footsteps。字面意思就是"踩着某人的脚印走"——公园里的护林员叔叔，可不就是踩着恐龙的脚印，一步一步在找它嘛。听听男主播的讲解和例句。

男生: Here's today's idiom: "follow in someone's footsteps." If you follow in someone's footsteps, you do the same job or the same things as a person who came before you — just like walking in the footprints they left in the ground.

The rangers in our story followed in the dinosaur's footsteps, step by step, footprint by footprint! Here is an example: "Lucy's grandmother was a great scientist, and Lucy wants to follow in her footsteps one day." Whose footsteps would you like to follow in?
