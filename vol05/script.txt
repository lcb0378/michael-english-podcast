男生: Now meet the mantis shrimp, a small sea animal with a very big superpower. It is famous for two things: a punch faster than a speeding bullet, and truly amazing eyes.

Your eyes have three kinds of color sensors. The mantis shrimp has many, many more. Scientists from Belgium and Germany studied seventeen light pigments inside its eyes. Pigments are the tiny parts that catch light and turn it into pictures for the brain.

They found something nobody expected: four of the pigments work like tiny light switches. Red light can switch them on, and blue light can switch them back off. Other pigments answer to blue and orange light. With all these clever switches, the shrimp can see colors of light that our eyes cannot see at all.

Why does this matter to us? Doctors dream of tools that use light to switch tiny parts of the body on and off — a little like fixing a machine with a flashlight. The shrimp's eyes may give scientists new ideas for such tools. Nature, once again, built it first!
