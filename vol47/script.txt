男生: Hello, young explorers, and welcome back! Today we celebrate a grandmother who proves adventure has no age limit, and the super-strong heart of the tallest animal on Earth. Ready? Let's go!

女生: 先说旅行奶奶。她叫 Joy，大家都叫她 Grandma Joy。她拿到第一本护照的时候，已经九十一岁了。在那之前，她连大山和大海都没见过。后来，她和孙子一起出发，越走越远。竖起耳朵听好啦。

男生: Joy Ryan, known as Grandma Joy, did not get her first passport until she was 91. Before that, she had never seen a mountain or an ocean. Then her grandson Brad took her camping in the mountains, and that one small trip changed everything. Together, they visited all 63 national parks in the United States, and Joy became the oldest person ever to do that. But they did not stop there. From North America, they went on to Europe, Asia, Africa, South America, and Australia, and finally Antarctica. At 96, Joy had set foot on all seven continents, with her grandson beside her. Her approach to life is simple: never say you are too old to try. A big trip can be expensive, but Joy shows us it does not have to cost a lot to be precious. She has no diploma in travel: she just went, one step at a time, and discovered the world.

女生: 从奶奶的环球旅行，我们抬头看看非洲大草原上最高的动物——长颈鹿。它的脑袋离心脏那么远，血液是怎么送上去的？它的心脏到底有多厉害？快听！

男生: Now let's meet the tallest animal on Earth: the giraffe. A giraffe can stand nearly six metres tall, its head high above its heart, almost like a brain living at a high altitude. So how does blood get up there? The answer is one amazing aspect of the giraffe: its heart. A giraffe's heart weighs about 11 kilograms, as heavy as a big dog, and measures about 60 centimetres long. Its walls are thick muscle tissue, and every beat sends a strong pulse of liquid blood up the long neck. Scientists who conducted studies found that a giraffe's blood pressure is about twice as high as ours. But the giraffe has a clever trick. When it lowers its head to drink, one-way valves and a net of tiny blood vessels slow the blood and protect the brain, so the giraffe never faints. Another surprise: a mother giraffe is pregnant for about 15 months, and her baby drops nearly two metres to the ground at birth, then stands up within an hour! Even a tiny sparrow's heart beats fast, but no heart works as hard against gravity as a giraffe's. Look for the two little horns on a giraffe's forehead next time you see one: they are called ossicones.

男生: Today's idiom is "cry over spilled milk." It means: do not waste time feeling sad about a small mistake you cannot undo. Imagine you knock over a glass of milk. The milk is already on the floor. Crying will not put it back in the glass! So we say: don't cry over spilled milk. Just clean it up and move on. For example: I lost the game yesterday, but there is no use crying over spilled milk; I will practise and try again tomorrow. Grandma Joy lives this way: if a plan goes wrong on a trip, she laughs, finds a new plan, and keeps going. Try it this week: when something small goes wrong, say, "No crying over spilled milk!"
