男生: Can you imagine a bee playing soccer? Scientists did more than imagine — they actually trained bumblebees to push a tiny ball into a tiny goal!

Here is how it worked. The scientists built a mini soccer field with a little ball and a small goal. When a bee pushed the ball into the goal — GOAL! — it got a sweet drink of sugar water as a prize. Bees love sweet things, so they learned fast.

But here is the amazing part. The bees did not just learn by trying again and again. They learned by watching! A "teacher bee" showed them how to push the ball, and the student bees copied it. Even cooler, some bees found a shorter, smarter path to the goal than the teacher showed them. They were not just copying — they were thinking!

The most surprising moment came later. When the scientists tested the bees the next day, the bees still remembered how to score — they had not forgotten overnight! For a brain smaller than a grain of rice, that is like remembering a whole dance routine after seeing it only once.

This little experiment tells us something big: even tiny insect brains can learn new tricks and pass them on. Who knows what else small brains can do?

女生: 从足球场，我们潜入海底。接下来这位有八条腿，它睡觉的时候，皮肤会像放电影一样变颜色——科学家说，它可能在做梦！

男生: Now let's dive under the sea and meet the octopus — the eight-armed genius of the ocean.

When you sleep, your eyes move quickly — scientists call this dream sleep. Octopuses have something similar! When an octopus falls into deep sleep, its skin starts to flash and change: stripes, spots, waves of color, all dancing across its body. It looks like it is watching a movie behind closed eyes.

Scientists watched sleeping octopuses very carefully and recorded their brain activity too. During these colorful moments, the octopus's brain looks surprisingly awake — almost as if it is reliving the day, maybe practicing how to catch a crab or escape from danger, all inside a dream.

And here is a fun thought: octopuses have three hearts and blue blood. With a body that strange, who knows what their dreams look like? Maybe they dream in eight directions at once!

So the next time you see an octopus nap at the aquarium, take a good look at its skin. You might be watching someone else's dream!
