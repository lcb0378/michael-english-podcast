女生: 欢迎来到 Michael 的早听晚读！我是 Cherry。今天有两个了不起的主角：一个是五十多万块的超级拼图，另一个是腿断了还能再长回来的小动物——墨西哥钝口螈。准备好了吗？出发！

男生: Hello, young explorers, and welcome back to Michael's Morning Listening! Today we meet two awesome record-breakers: a jigsaw puzzle with more than half a million pieces, and a little animal that can regrow its own leg. Ready? Let's go!

女生: 先说拼图。你家里拼过一千块的拼图吗？那已经够难了。可有一群学生，拼了一幅五十多万块的拼图，拼好以后比一间大教室还大！他们是怎么做到的？竖起耳朵听好啦。

男生: In a big stadium in Vietnam, 1,600 students worked together on the biggest jigsaw puzzle ever made. It had 551,232 pieces! When it was finished, the picture was a beautiful lotus flower, nearly 15 meters wide and 23 meters long — bigger than a classroom, bigger than a house!

The puzzle was so big that nobody could work on it all at once. It was cut into 3,132 small sections, and each team worked on one section. Each piece had only one valid place. A piece in the wrong place, and the picture would not work. With patience and teamwork, the students fitted section after section together. After 17 hours of work, the last piece clicked into place. Imagine the cheers!

Even the biggest puzzle you can buy in a shop is much smaller: a puzzle with 54,000 pieces, showing more than 50 famous paintings. That is still awesome, but the stadium puzzle is ten times bigger. People enjoy puzzles because every piece is a small mystery. So remember: no puzzle is too big if you cut it into small parts and work together.

女生: 从拼图的大体育场，我们潜入墨西哥的湖水里。那里住着一种长得像小恐龙的动物，中文叫它“六角恐龙”。它最厉害的本事是：腿断了，还能再长一条新的！这是真的吗？快听！

男生: Meet the axolotl, a salamander from Mexico. In Chinese it is called the "six-horned dinosaur", because of the six feathery gills on its head. But it is not a dinosaur at all — it is a salamander that keeps its baby looks as a grown-up and lives in the water all its life. Its delicate, bright pink gills wave in the water like tiny feathers.

Here is the difference between us and the axolotl. When we get a bad cut, our body makes a scar. When an axolotl loses a leg, its body does something amazing: it grows a brand-new leg, with new bones, muscles, and even the same skin patterns. It can do this again and again. It can also regrow parts of its heart and its spinal cord!

How does it work? When a leg is lost, the cells under the skin are exposed. Instead of making a scar, those cells change back into young, growing cells and form a soft lump called a blastema. The blastema is like a builder's workshop: it slowly builds the missing leg, piece by piece. Scientists study the axolotl to learn how it recovers so perfectly. One day, this knowledge might help doctors help people recover from injuries.

In the wild, axolotls live only in the lakes near Mexico City, and they are in danger because their lakes are shrinking. Scientists are working hard to protect them.

女生: 学知识时间到！今天的俚语，和拼图卡住时、还有遇到难事时都需要的一样东西有关：bite the bullet。字面意思是“咬住子弹”，说的是硬着头皮去做一件难事。听听男主播的讲解和例句。

男生: Here's today's idiom: 'bite the bullet.' When you bite the bullet, you do something difficult or unpleasant — you just face it bravely! Here's an example: 'Tom lost the instructions for his puzzle, but he bit the bullet and started with the edge pieces.' When did you last bite the bullet?

女生: 今天的故事都听完啦？我们见了五十多万块的超级拼图，又认识了能再生的小恐龙。现在轮到你们动脑筋啦，听听男主播的问题，想好了可以说给爸爸妈妈听。
