男生: Our first hero is Gitanjali Rao, an eleven-year-old primary school girl from Colorado. When she was ten, she watched the news when a water crisis erupted in a city called Flint. The water there was not safe: it had lead in it, a poison that can wound the brains of young children. Gitanjali was sad, and she was brave too. It takes courage to look at a big problem and say, "I can help!"
She began to learn everything about water. She used a search engine to read about tiny tubes called carbon nanotubes. Then she built a small device and gave it a beautiful name: Tethys, after the Greek goddess of fresh water. You dip a small stick into the water, and the device sends the answer to a phone app in seconds. To everyone's delight, it worked!
She took her invention to a big contest for young scientists, and she won the top prize — America's Top Young Scientist — and twenty-five thousand dollars. One girl, one idea, and cleaner water for everyone. Isn't that wonderful?

女生: 从实验室潜入大海！第二位主角是一种比你的小指头还小的水母。别的动物越长越大、越长越老，它却能把自己变回"小时候"，重新再长大一次。这是怎么做到的呢？

男生: Now dive into the sea for our second hero: a jellyfish smaller than your little finger. Its name is Turritopsis dohrnii, but scientists call it the immortal jellyfish. Do not worry — it is not really immortal. It can still be eaten by a bigger fish, or die of hunger. But it has one magic trick no other animal has.
When this jellyfish gets old, sick, or hurt, it does something amazing. Its soft flesh sinks to the sea floor, and its cells change their jobs. The cells of its bell and arms turn back into young cells, called a polyp. The polyp is like a baby jellyfish, and it grows up all over again. Its old body fades away, and a new life begins.
By coincidence, scientists found this little animal in the warm sea near Italy, and they could not believe their eyes. A creature that can be young again! It is not dead and alive again like a ghost story. It turns back time, with its own cells.
So remember: this jellyfish is no superhero — it just knows how to start again.

男生: Here's today's idiom: "when pigs fly." Pigs cannot fly! So when someone says "when pigs fly," they mean "never!" Listen to this: Lily wanted a pet pig. Her father laughed and said, "You can have a pig when pigs fly!" Lily wrote the words in her notebook with her pen. Then she put on her father's big hat and danced around the room. "Look, Dad!" she shouted. "The pig can wait — but I can fly!"
